SermonDesk help

Thank you for trying this early. You will find things I have not, and I want to hear about them.

Installing on Windows

  1. Press Download for Windows at https://sarangbi.co. It fetches the newest installer straight away; the same file is also listed at https://github.com/DLSarangbi/sermondesk-releases/releases/latest.
  2. Run it. The installer is signed, and the publisher shown is Davis Lor, which is me.

If Windows still shows a blue Windows protected your PC box, it is SmartScreen weighing a signature it has not seen often yet; More info, then Run anyway gets past it, and I would like to hear that it happened. Anything else, any warning naming a different publisher, stop and write to me.

Installing on a Mac

It needs a Mac with Apple Silicon: an M1 or later, which is every Mac sold since late 2020, running macOS 13 or newer. (The dictation engine has no build for Intel Macs, so there is no Intel version; an Intel Mac refuses the download with its own message rather than installing an app whose Dictate button could never work.)

  1. Press Download for Mac at https://sarangbi.co. It fetches the newest disk image straight away; the same file is also listed at https://github.com/DLSarangbi/sermondesk-releases/releases/latest.
  2. Open the downloaded SermonDesk.dmg and drag SermonDesk into the Applications folder beside it. Then eject the disk image and open SermonDesk from Applications, or from Launchpad.
  3. The first time, macOS says the app was downloaded from the internet and asks whether you are sure. Press Open. The app is signed and notarized by Apple, and the developer named is Davis Lor, which is me.

If the Mac instead says the app is damaged, or that Apple could not check it for malicious software, stop and write to me: that means something went wrong on my side, not yours, and I want to know. The first time you press Dictate, macOS asks whether SermonDesk may use the microphone, and says in that dialog exactly what happens to your voice: it stays on the computer.

The first thing you will be asked

Where your sermons should live. Choose iCloud Drive, Google Drive, OneDrive, or Dropbox if you use one: your sermons are then backed up and synced across your machines on your own account, with nothing stored by me. The welcome screen shows each service as a card; the ones set up on your computer are live, and one click makes a SermonDesk folder inside. A plain folder of your own is always there too.

The app opens in your computer's own language when it is one of the fourteen it speaks, and in English otherwise; the welcome screen has a language menu in its corner, so the first thing you can change is the words you are reading. If you do not use any of those, any folder works. Everything is a plain file either way. Every time the app opens it shows this screen again, with your folder named and Continue beside it, so you can go straight in or change where the sermons live first: Connect beside a service found on your computer moves your sermons into it, Disconnect moves them back out to a folder you choose, and Use switches to a SermonDesk folder already synced there, as on a second computer. The old folder goes to the Recycle Bin, or the Trash on a Mac, not away. The same is under Settings, then Folder.

More than one computer

Say you write on a Mac and preach from a Windows laptop.

  1. On the Mac, put your sermons in Dropbox, or iCloud Drive, Google Drive, or OneDrive. SermonDesk offers this at the welcome screen.
  2. On the laptop, install Dropbox and sign in. Your sermons arrive by themselves.
  3. Open SermonDesk on the laptop and press Use beside Dropbox.

That's it. Write on any of them, and the others have it.

One thing to set on each computer: keep the folder downloaded. iCloud Drive's Optimise Mac Storage, and OneDrive's Files On-Demand on Windows, may leave only a placeholder of a sermon on the computer to free space. The app lists such a sermon as in iCloud and asks for it back when it starts, but at a church without wifi it is not there to read. In the Finder, right-click the SermonDesk folder and choose Keep Downloaded; in Windows Explorer, right-click it and choose Always keep on this device.

Your subscription key

Writing needs a subscription key. Click Enter key… in the notice above the page, paste it in, and that is the whole setup: it is checked once online and then works offline. Preaching from the podium needs it too. Everything else, opening, searching, and printing, never needs the key at all. If you have no key, Buy a key in the same notice opens the checkout in your browser, and the key arrives by email a moment after you pay. If you were sent a discount code with this guide, it goes in at checkout.

Your key works on up to three computers — your desk, your laptop, wherever you write. If you ever need to move it off a machine, open Settings (its button on the toolbar, or Ctrl+comma; ⌘, on a Mac), go to Subscription, and choose Deactivate this computer, which frees a slot for the next one.

What is in it

  • Write sermons in blocks: point, scripture, story, application, reflection, private note
  • Quote scripture from the Bible you already read: paste a copied verse and it is filed with its reference and version filled in, or type one in with /scripture
  • Search everything you have ever written, find every sermon touching a passage, and keep a saved search for the ones you come back to
  • A preaching log on every sermon: each time it was preached, where, and how long it ran, written by the podium when you mark it preached
  • Preach from a fullscreen podium screen with a timer
  • Print handouts, including a large-print preset for anyone who has stopped being able to follow along
  • Stories, a place for the stories you tell, which says where you have already told each one
  • Import your existing sermons from Word files, or a whole folder of Google Docs exported through Takeout. Headings become points, bold and italic survive, and everything arrives as drafts for you to look over

What is not in it yet

  • The Mac version is new. It passes every check the Windows version passes, on a real Apple Silicon Mac, and is signed and notarized by Apple; but it shipped before anyone had written a whole sermon in it. Windows has had more hands on it. If the Mac does anything the Windows version would not, that is exactly the report I want.
  • Nothing has been through a real Sunday yet except my own testing.

Your sermons are yours

Every sermon is a plain, readable file in the folder you chose. You can open one in Notepad, or TextEdit, right now and read it. If this app disappears tomorrow, or you decide you hate it, nothing of yours is trapped: the files are just there.

Nothing is uploaded. Nothing is sent anywhere. On its own, the app makes two kinds of network request, both of which can fail without affecting anything: a subscription check when it starts, and an update check when it starts and every six hours while it stays open. One more happens only when you ask for it: the first time you press Dictate, the voice model is fetched and kept. Spelling uses the checker built into Windows, or into macOS, so it needs nothing from the network. Go offline and nothing changes for a month. Every request, and what is in it, is on the privacy page at https://sarangbi.co/privacy.html.

What would help most

Not bug lists: situations. The things worth hearing about:

  • Anything you lost. A sentence, a block, a whole sermon. This matters more than everything else combined. Tell me what you did immediately before.
  • Anything at the podium. The podium is the part that cannot fail. If it did anything strange mid-sermon, I want to know exactly what.
  • Anything you expected to be there and wasn't, especially something your old software did that you miss.
  • The handout. If you printed one, did it look like something you would hand to a congregation?

Rough notes are fine. "I tried to X and gave up" is a genuinely useful report. Send it all to davislor@sarangbi.co, or just reply to the message this guide came with.

If something goes wrong

Your sermons are on disk in the folder you chose: that is always the first reassurance. Nothing the app does can take them away, and nothing it stores elsewhere matters.

The app keeps a search index separately from your sermons. If search ever behaves oddly, closing and reopening the app rebuilds that index from your files.

If a save ever fails, because the folder is read-only or a sync service is holding the file, the line under the page says so and your words are kept on this computer; they go into the folder with the next save that succeeds, and if you had closed the app, the next launch offers them above the page.

The app also keeps a log of what it did and when, with nothing of your sermons in it, and it never leaves your computer unless you send it. Settings, then General, has Show the log at the bottom, and Write to us beside it, which opens a message with the version and your system already filled in. Say what you did just before, and attach the log if you can.
