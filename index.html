Your sermons are files, in a folder you choose.

One readable file per sermon, in a folder you choose. Keep that folder in iCloud Drive, Google Drive, OneDrive, or Dropbox and you have backup and sync on your own account, with nothing stored by us. A file written by any version opens in every later one.

Bring the sermons you already have: a Word file imports as a sermon, its passage read from the opening line in whatever language it was written. Going the other way, print the manuscript, the outline and the handout or save them as PDF, send a sermon to Word, and export the whole library as plain text at any time, no licence asked.

The library knows what a passage is.

Type Romans 8 and get every sermon that touched it, even one that quoted a single verse. Open a sermon and see what else touches its chapter, where else you told that story, and the rest of its series. A map of the whole canon shows the chapters you have preached and the books you never have.

Fifty tags do not become a wall.

Tags complete as you type them, so a slip of the shift key never makes a twin. Put them in groups and each group is one chip in the library, opening to a short list with counts: pick one from each and the groups combine. What a sermon is for, a funeral, a wedding, Easter, is a word in the byline rather than a tag, with its own chip. And a tag is a place: press one and see every sermon that carries it in the order they were preached, the tags that travel with it, and the chapters it came from.
